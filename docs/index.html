Fernwood checkout, reviewed by five seats

Product management, engineering, content design, product design, and the user each review the same four-screen flow from their own rubric. The useful part is where they disagree.

Fictional. Fernwood is an invented plant shop, and this flow was written to test the skill. Nothing here refers to a real product.

The flow

Mobile web, pre-ship, launching in two weeks. Fixed payment provider, three languages, guest checkout required. The author was unsure about the delivery date picker.

  1. 1 · CartPromo code open by default. Two equal filled buttons. Shipping “calculated at next step.”
  2. 2 · DeliveryTuesday and Thursday dates only. “Invalid input” on any bad field.
  3. 3 · PaymentLive 10:00 countdown. Shipping fee appears here first. “Payment failed.”
  4. 4 · Confirmation“Order placed! 🌿” No date, no email note.

What’s working

Protect these while fixing the rest.

  • Guest checkout with no forced account. It removes the biggest cause of abandonment, and it’s the requirement most often dropped in the last sprint.
  • Only offering real delivery days. Honest about how the business works. The gap is the missing explanation, not the restriction.
  • Four screens with one job each. The structure is right, so most fixes happen inside screens, not to the flow.

Where they disagree

Each tension is one of three kinds: answerable with data, a real tradeoff someone must own, or a false conflict that one small change resolves.

Answerable

The reservation timer: urgency versus a screen reader and an expiry bug

Product managementA live reservation creates urgency, and urgency lifts completion on stock-limited items like live plants.
Engineering and the userIt has no defined behavior at 0:00 mid-payment, and it announces every second to a screen reader.

Resolution: Test with and without it on a slice of traffic, comparing completion and oversold-order rate. If it stays, freeze it during authorization, warn at 2:00, and add a one-tap extend. Product management owns the call.

False conflict

Error messages: six strings in three languages versus “Invalid input”

EngineeringEvery error variant is a string in three languages, forever.
Content design“Invalid input” and “Payment failed.” are why customers contact support.

Resolution: Most of the cost is the API returning no reasons. Return a reason code per field (about a day of backend work), then write four short messages: required, format, out of area, declined. Nobody loses.

Real tradeoff

Shipping on the cart: no surprise versus a fee that can’t be known yet

Product managementA $12.95 surprise on the last screen is the biggest abandonment risk in the flow.
EngineeringShipping depends on postcode and box size, and neither exists at the cart.

Decision for product management: a flat estimate on the cart (“from $9.95, final after you enter your address”), free-shipping threshold messaging, or moving the address step first. Each trades accuracy, effort, and flow order. Design should propose which keeps it at four screens.

False conflict

Cart buttons: one calm primary versus keeping “Continue shopping” visible

Product management“Continue shopping” recovers customers who aren’t ready to buy.
Product designTwo equal filled buttons split attention at the moment of commitment.

Resolution: Make “Continue shopping” an outline or text button. It stays visible and recoverable while “Proceed to checkout” is the only filled button. Confirm by comparing the click ratio before and after.

If you fix three things

Ranked, and specific enough to start today.

  1. Define what the timer does, and make it accessible.Freeze it during card authorization, announce only at 2:00 and 0:30, add a one-tap extend, and decide what happens at 0:00. The only issue that both loses money and blocks a user.
  2. Show shipping before the payment screen.Even as “from $9.95, final after you enter your address.” It removes the top abandonment driver and makes the total believable.
  3. Fix the dead-end messages.Replace “Payment failed.” and “Invalid input” with specific, actionable ones backed by field-level reasons. Add the delivery date and the email line to the confirmation.
